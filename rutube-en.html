Rutube Premium subscription checkout flow

Overview

A redesign of the plan selection, payment, and subscription management pages for a service with a million-strong audience.

91.7SUS out of 100
6.3SEQ out of 7
Problem

Users were abandoning subscription checkout before reaching payment: plans were described vaguely, auto-renewal terms were hidden in fine print, and there was no sense of completion after payment.

Target audience

Core audience — 25–34: has disposable income, mature with digital products, already used to living on subscriptions. But within it — not one single behavior pattern.

Method

In-depth interviews with audience representatives showed: the same action — subscribing — hides different motives, fears, and expectations.

Controller

When I open the subscription page and don't understand the terms, I want to find clear information about the price, charge date, and cancellation, so I don't feel like I've been deceived.

When I've just paid for the subscription, I want to immediately see the next charge date and quickly find subscription management, so I stay in control of the situation.

Hypothesis

If the subscription terms are made clear upfront — an explicit plan comparison, a visible charge date, a sense of completion after payment — users will stop abandoning checkout, and the process itself will become noticeably simpler. Validated through moderated testing of a clickable prototype: the same respondents as in the research phase — which let them compare the old Rutube flow and the new prototype from their own experience.

Insights

The flow confirmed the hypothesis: both archetypes' pain points are closed, success criteria met at 100%.

Archetypes work as a frame, but not a rigid one: behavior can diverge from the original classification depending on context.

SEQ 6.3 out of 7, SUS 91.7 out of 100 — high simplicity and ease of use.

Two growth points found: the error screen reads as a dead end, and the cancel button on the success screen is visually weak.

Transparent terms and explicit control over the subscription removed the main fear — hidden charges.

Results
The case went through the full cycle: from research to a system ready for development.
What was done
  • Built a research base: interviews, competitive analysis, MoSCoW prioritization
  • Formed two behavioral archetypes based on observed patterns
  • Designed the information architecture and CJM
  • Drew key screens: plan selection, payment, success, error, subscription management
  • Built a two-tier token system and a UI kit of 7 sections
  • Ran usability testing: hypothesis confirmed, key archetype pain points closed (SUS 91.7, SEQ 6.3)
